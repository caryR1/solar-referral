# Solar by GemzOnline — Episode 2 Script (first draft)

**Positioning** (per the Solar Gmail draft, locked 2026-09-06): Episode 1 is the trust-building channel intro; Episode 2 is "the clickbait play" — a real Warning/insider-secret angle, backed by genuine reveal content (not manufactured hype), released close behind Episode 1 so intro earns trust and this one earns clicks.

**Working titles** (pick one, or use as a starting point — title/thumbnail/intro need to match exactly per the Holy Trifecta congruence principle):
- "The Real Reason Solar Companies Don't Want You Reading Your Contract"
- "3 Clauses Hiding In Your Solar Contract (And What They Actually Mean)"
- "What Your Solar Contract Isn't Telling You"

**Content basis**: these are genuine, well-documented issues in the solar industry generally (not specific to any one company, and not fabricated) — safe to present as real reveal content that backs up a punchy title honestly, consistent with the channel's "no overstated claims" standard.

1. **Escalator clauses** — some solar leases/PPAs (power purchase agreements) include an annual price increase built into the contract, often 1-3% per year. Ironic given the whole pitch is "escape rising utility bills" — homeowners can end up locked into their own rising price, just a different one.
2. **Transferability at home sale** — a solar loan, lease, or PPA attached to the house can complicate or delay closing when the home is sold; some buyers won't qualify to take over the agreement, which can become a real obstacle at exactly the wrong time.
3. **Production guarantee loopholes** — "guaranteed output" language often comes with exclusions (shading, weather, panel degradation assumptions) that can make the guarantee much weaker in practice than it sounds in the pitch.
4. **Long contract terms** — leases and PPAs commonly run 20-25 years, longer than many people stay in a home, which is part of why point 2 matters.
5. **UCC lien filings** — some financed systems result in a lien filed against the home, similar to a mortgage lien, which needs to be understood (and disclosed) up front, not discovered later.

**Structure**:
1. Fast, punchy hook naming a specific real red flag (not vague fear-mongering) — earns the title within the first 10-15 seconds.
2. Quick reminder of who's talking (independent consultant, one line, not the full episode-1 intro again).
3. Walk through 2-3 of the red flags above with enough specificity to be genuinely useful, not just alarming.
4. What to actually do about it (ask your installer directly about escalators/transferability/lien before signing — turns the reveal into actionable advice, not just anxiety).
5. Referral program mention — one line only, pointing to the description, consistent with Episode 1's approach.
6. Close + tease episode 3.
